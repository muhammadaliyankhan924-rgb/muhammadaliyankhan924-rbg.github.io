Online Safety and Privacy

How to Create Strong Passwords and Protect Your Accounts

Practical steps to make your online accounts harder to break into.

Your email, social media, shopping, and financial accounts may contain valuable personal information. Strong passwords and additional security checks can reduce the risk of unauthorized access.

1. Make Passwords Long and Unique

Long passwords are generally harder to guess. Use a different password for every important account. If one service is compromised, unique passwords help prevent attackers from using the same credentials elsewhere.

2. Try a Passphrase

A passphrase combines several unrelated words and can be easier to remember than a short string. Make it sufficiently long and unique. Avoid famous quotes, common phrases, personal details, and predictable substitutions.

3. Protect Your Email Account First

Your email account is especially important because it may be used to reset passwords for other services. Give it a unique password, enable additional security protections, and keep its recovery email and phone details up to date.

4. Use a Password Manager

A reputable password manager can generate and store unique passwords so you do not need to memorize every one. Protect it with a strong master password and enable its available security features. Only install a manager from its official website or a trusted app store.

5. Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication adds another step when signing in. Depending on the service, options may include an authenticator app, a security key, or a verification code. Prefer phishing-resistant security keys or passkeys when supported and practical.

6. Learn About Passkeys

Passkeys let supported services authenticate you using your device's security features. They can be more resistant to phishing than traditional passwords. Use official account settings to create and manage passkeys, and keep your recovery options available.

7. Never Share Verification Codes

Someone may pretend to be a bank employee, support agent, or friend to persuade you to reveal a one-time code. Never share verification codes or approve sign-in requests that you did not initiate.

8. Recognize Fake Login Pages

Check the website address carefully before entering a password. Scammers can copy the appearance of real websites. Instead of following unexpected login links, open the official app or type the service's known address yourself.

9. Review Account Activity

Where available, check recent sign-ins, active sessions, and connected devices. Sign out unfamiliar sessions and change your password if you suspect unauthorized access. Follow the service's official recovery instructions if you cannot sign in.

10. Change Passwords When Needed

You do not necessarily need to change a strong password on a fixed schedule if it remains secure. Change it promptly if it was exposed, reused on a compromised service, shared accidentally, or involved in suspicious activity.

What If Your Password Is Stolen?

  1. Using a trusted device, change the affected password immediately.
  2. Change the password anywhere else you reused it.
  3. Sign out other sessions and review your recovery details.
  4. Enable two-factor authentication if it is not already enabled.
  5. Contact your bank or the relevant service if financial or sensitive information may be affected.
  6. If you cannot access the account, use the service's official account recovery process.

Your Account Security Checklist

Use this checklist to review your account security:
  • Every important account has its own password.
  • My email account has a unique password.
  • Two-factor authentication is enabled where available.
  • I never share verification codes with other people.
  • I know how to recover my important accounts.
  • I review unfamiliar sign-in alerts and account activity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a password strong?

A strong password is sufficiently long, unique to that account, and difficult to guess. A password manager can help generate and store random passwords securely.

Should I use the same password for multiple accounts?

No. Reusing passwords increases risk because a password exposed by one service may be tried on your other accounts.

Are password managers safe to use?

A reputable password manager can improve security by helping you create and store unique passwords. Choose a trusted provider, protect your main account, and follow its security recommendations.

Is two-factor authentication worth enabling?

Yes. It adds another layer of protection if someone learns your password. Use the strongest practical authentication method supported by the service.

When should I change my password?

Change it if you believe it has been exposed, if a service reports a breach affecting your account, or if you notice suspicious activity. Also replace reused passwords when one of the services is compromised.

Final Thoughts

The most important habits are simple: use unique passwords, secure your email account, enable two-factor authentication, and stay alert to fake login pages. A password manager can make these habits easier to maintain. Review your account security periodically and use official recovery options if something goes wrong.
