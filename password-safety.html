How to Create Strong Passwords and Protect Your Accounts

Simple steps to make your online accounts harder to break into.

Your email, social media, and financial accounts may contain valuable personal information. Strong passwords and extra security checks can help reduce the risk of someone accessing them without permission.

1. Make Passwords Long and Unique

Long passwords are generally harder to guess. Use a different password for every important account. If one service is compromised, unique passwords help prevent attackers from using the same credentials elsewhere.

2. Try a Passphrase

A passphrase combines several unrelated words and can be easier to remember than a short, complicated string. Make it sufficiently long and unique, and avoid famous quotes, common phrases, personal details, or predictable substitutions.

3. Never Reuse Your Email Password

Your email account is especially important because it may be used to reset passwords for other services. Give it a unique password and enable additional security protections. Keep its recovery email and phone details up to date.

4. Use a Password Manager

A reputable password manager can generate and store unique passwords so you do not need to memorize every one. Protect the manager with a strong master password and enable its available security features. Only install it from a trusted source.

5. Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication adds another step when signing in. Depending on the service, options may include an authenticator app, a security key, or a verification code. Prefer phishing-resistant security keys or passkeys when supported and practical.

6. Learn About Passkeys

Passkeys let supported services authenticate you using your device's built-in security features. They can be more resistant to phishing than traditional passwords. Use the official account settings to create and manage passkeys, and keep recovery options available.

7. Never Share Verification Codes

Someone may pretend to be a bank employee, platform support agent, or friend to persuade you to reveal a one-time code. Do not share verification codes or approve sign-in prompts that you did not initiate.

8. Recognize Fake Login Pages

Before entering a password, check the website address carefully. Scammers can copy the appearance of real websites. Instead of following unexpected login links, open the official app or type the service's known address yourself.

9. Review Account Activity

Where available, check recent sign-ins, active sessions, and connected devices. Sign out unfamiliar sessions and change your password if you suspect unauthorized access. Follow the service's official recovery instructions if you cannot sign in.

10. Change Passwords When Needed

You do not necessarily need to change a strong password on a fixed schedule if it remains secure. Change it promptly if it was exposed, reused on a compromised service, shared accidentally, or involved in suspicious activity.

What If Your Password Is Stolen?

  1. From a trusted device, change the affected password immediately.
  2. Change it anywhere else you reused it.
  3. Sign out other sessions and review recovery details.
  4. Enable two-factor authentication if it is not already enabled.
  5. Contact your bank or the relevant service if financial or sensitive information may be affected.

Final Thoughts

The most important habits are simple: use unique passwords, keep your email account secure, enable two-factor authentication, and stay alert to fake login pages. A password manager can make these habits easier to maintain.
